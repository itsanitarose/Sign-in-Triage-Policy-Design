
# 04. Shared Tablet Sign-In

## The risk

A managed, compliant tablet meets Northwind’s device security requirements, but it does not tell us which employee is using an active portal session. If a portal session stays open at handover, the next person could access patient data under the previous user’s session.

## The method

Each front-desk staff member has their own account and their own hardware key. They sign in on the company-managed tablet at the desk. Staff do not need a personal phone to sign in.

- The tablet is managed and compliant.
- The account is the person's own, not fd-clinic12.
- MFA is the hardware key. No authenticator app. No SMS.
- Each staff member keeps their assigned hardware key. Keys are not shared or left with the tablet.
- The portal session ends before handover. The next person signs in with their own account.
- A lost key is replaced through a controlled recovery process. It is not a spare key everyone uses.

## The trade-off

Stronger accountability requires a fresh sign-in at each handover. That adds a step during a busy clinic period. A shared account is faster to use, but the sign-in log cannot reliably identify which staff member used it.

## The check

After the first staff member signs out, the next person opens the portal before signing in. They should see a sign-in prompt, not the previous person's patient information. If they see the previous session, the handover did not clear it. Run this check before retiring the shared account and removing IT-2201. Then confirm the next employee can sign in using their own account and hardware key.

## What this replaces

#9 and #10 failed when MFA timed out. #11 succeeded under the IT-2201 exemption. Once the shared account is disabled, the sign-ins recorded in #9, #10 and #11 would be blocked. Staff would instead use their individual accounts and hardware keys.
